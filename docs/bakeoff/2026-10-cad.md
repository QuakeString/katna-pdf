# CAD prototype: scenes for vector-heavy pages (2026-10-17)

This is the Phase 0 CAD prototype from `docs/PLAN.md` §2.5, steps 1, 2, 3
and part of 4. Page 1 is read once into a scene. Each tile then draws only
what a grid index finds inside it.

- **Code:** `crates/katna-pdf-render/src/scene.rs`.
- **Benchmark:** `katna-pdf-bench --cad FILE…`.
- **Check against hayro:** `katna-pdf-bench --scene-check FILE…`.

## What it does

- **Detect.** A page whose own content stream builds at least 50,000 path
  segments counts as vector-heavy (`is_vector_heavy`). Other pages never
  pay for a scene. When the content is one Flate stream, only as much of
  it is inflated as the count needs.
- **Read once.** The page is read into a scene. A page that only draws
  plain paths (numbers, path and painting operators, line styles, grey
  and RGB colours, clips) is read by our own quick reader
  (`scene/plain.rs`), which feeds the scene exactly what hayro-interpret
  would. Any other page, or one with annotations, is read by
  hayro-interpret. A single Flate stream is inflated on a second thread
  a megabyte at a time while our reader reads what has arrived, so the
  two overlap. Each path's
  points go into flat arrays, colours and strokes are shared, and clips
  form a tree. Every item gets its bounding box in page points, with its
  clip applied.
- **Index.** Every box goes into a uniform grid over the page, about 64
  items a cell (`grid.rs`). An item that spans more than 16 cells, such
  as a sheet's border, goes into one shared list. The grid is built in
  two passes over the boxes. It replaced an `rstar` R-tree on
  2026-10-18, whose bulk load took about 0.23 s of the 1M sheet's first
  view.
- **Draw a tile.** The grid is asked for the tile's box, and the items it
  finds are drawn in page order with vello_cpu. Clips are opened and
  closed only as they change. Hairlines are kept at least a pixel wide, as
  hayro draws them.
- **Level of detail.** An item smaller than ¾ pixel on both sides is
  drawn as a dot of its colour instead of being stroked or filled.
- **Quick line drawer.** A tile in which every item is a dot or a line a
  pixel wide, clipped only by rectangles, is drawn by our own rasterizer
  (`scene/hairline.rs`) instead of vello_cpu. Zoomed out on a drawing,
  that is every tile. Each column a line crosses (each row, for a steep
  line) gets the line's coverage of its pixels, summed over the path so
  shared pixels count once, as vello's fill counts them. Curves are
  flattened, and round and square caps lengthen the ends of open paths.
  It costs about 1 µs an item where vello_cpu costs about 2.7 µs.
- **Fallback.** A page holding anything the scene cannot draw yet is drawn
  the usual way. That covers images, patterns and shadings, soft masks,
  blend modes, groups that change opacity, and Type 3 text.

`Hayro` uses scenes. `HayroPlain` never does; it is there so the two can be
compared.

## Speed

Each step draws a 1920 × 1080 view as 512-pixel tiles on all 4 cores of a
2.1 GHz Xeon. The steps are the sheet fitted to the view, then 100 %,
400 % and 3200 % around the sheet's middle. "Sheet" includes opening the
file and, for scenes, reading the page into the scene. Times are in ms;
**!** marks a miss of the §2.5 budget (600 ms to the first sheet, 250 ms
per zoom step).

| Sheet | Scene | View | Scene ms | Usual ms | Faster |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A1, 100k strokes | 100k items, 10 MB | sheet | 100 | 529 | 5.3× |
| | | 100 % | 41 | 490 ! | 12× |
| | | 400 % | 10 | 604 ! | 58× |
| | | 3200 % | 2 | 434 ! | 239× |
| A0, 400k strokes | 400k items, 41 MB | sheet | 319 | 2,080 ! | 6.5× |
| | | 100 % | 64 | 1,894 ! | 30× |
| | | 400 % | 11 | 1,762 ! | 155× |
| | | 3200 % | 3 | 1,950 ! | 687× |
| A0, 1M strokes | 1M items, 85 MB | sheet | 922 ! | 5,670 ! | 6.1× |
| | | 100 % | 125 | 5,649 ! | 45× |
| | | 400 % | 25 | 4,913 ! | 194× |
| | | 3200 % | 5 | 5,203 ! | 976× |

The first sheet views took 262, 846 and 2,142 ms before the grid and the
style cache below (2026-10-17), 184, 684 and 1,796 ms before our own
reader, 199, 591 and 1,565 ms before the quick line drawer, and 144,
436 and 1,176 ms before inflating while reading (all 2026-10-18).

The sheets come from `bakeoff/make_cad.py`.

For comparison, the engine bake-off (`docs/bakeoff/2026-10-engines.md`)
measured the 1M sheet differently, as one page at 150 % and one 1024²
tile at 800 %:

| Engine | Page at 150 % (ms) | 1024² tile at 800 % (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|
| PDFium | 12,444 | 1,492 |
| MuPDF | 21,170 | 5,262 |

A scene draws a whole 1920 × 1080 view at 400 % in 33 ms. Okular was not
measured, because it cannot be installed on the build machine.

What the numbers say:

- **Zooming in now costs what is on screen, not what is on the page.** At
  400 % and deeper, every sheet is far within the 250 ms budget, and
  deeper zoom is faster, as §2.5 intended.
- **The first full-sheet view of the 400k sheet is now within budget; the
  1M sheet's still misses.** On the 1M sheet, building the scene takes
  about 0.41 s (reading, with inflating alongside it, then the grid) and
  drawing every stroke about 0.35 s on 4 cores. Every stroke is larger than a pixel at
  that zoom, so the dots do not help. Where those times go:
  - **Next:** reading on more than one core, or a first view drawn
    from a coarser level while the rest is read.
  - **Inflating** the 1M sheet's 60 MB of content takes about 0.27 s
    with zlib-rs, now hidden behind reading. Before, it was done in one
    go, and the detector alone made hayro inflate the whole page.
  - **Reading.** Our own reader takes about 0.32 s for 4.8M operators,
    storing the items included. hayro took about 0.8 s: 0.25 s to
    tokenize, 0.4 s to interpret and 0.16 s to store.
  - **Drawing.** vello_cpu spends about 2.7 µs of CPU on each path,
    whatever its size on screen, so drawing at a quarter of the size is no
    faster. The quick line drawer above takes about 1 µs, most of it
    covering pixels, and draws the whole sheet in about 1 s of CPU where
    vello_cpu took 2.7 s. Before it, three cheaper ways through vello_cpu
    were tried and dropped: batching strokes of one style, stroking thin
    lines as quads of our own, and square caps for round ones. None saved more than about a tenth
    of the sheet view, and the last two changed the result more than the
    scene otherwise does.
  - **Whole pages in the reader.** The reader asks for a whole page as
    one region, which a scene drew on one core: about 1.1 s of drawing for
    the 1M sheet against the bench's 0.35 s of tiles. A large region is now
    drawn as bands on every core, 0.37 s (2026-10-18). The line drawer
    also stopped calling the maths library to round and allocating per
    stroke, about a tenth less CPU per view.
  - A precomputed overview, where strokes merge into tone, would cost a
    full draw to make, so it helps the views after the first, not the
    first one.
- **Memory** is about 85 bytes an item: 85 MB for 1M strokes, inside the
  300 MB budget.

## Correctness

`--scene-check` draws page 1 of each of the 994 files in the pdf.js test
corpus at 150 %, from a scene and the usual way, then compares the two.
This covers every page a scene can draw, vector-heavy or not.

- **Drawn from a scene:** 631 pages. Only one differs in more than 0.05 %
  of its pixels: `issue16316.pdf`, at 0.08 %.
- **Fallback:** 342 pages hold something a scene does not draw yet: 203
  images, 59 patterns, 27 groups, 24 Type 3 fonts, 16 blend modes and 13
  soft masks.
- **No page opened:** 19 files.
- **Since the grid:** the same check on 2026-10-18 gave every page the
  same result as with the R-tree.
- **Quick line drawer:** with it, the corpus check gives every page the
  same result as before, and the CAD sheets differ from the usual way in
  at most 0.16 % of pixels, mostly at the joins of polylines, which it
  does not draw. A test (`tests/hayro.rs`) checks a drawing of hairlines,
  polylines, curves and round caps.
- **Our own reader:** the check also reads each page our quick way and
  hayro's way and compares the two scenes item by item. The reader takes
  10 corpus pages and all three CAD sheets, and every one is identical to
  hayro's. It leaves every other page to hayro, mostly for text. A test
  (`tests/hayro.rs`) does the same for strokes, fills, curves, dashes,
  nested clips and odd numbers.
- **Slow:** 2 files, `bomb_giant.pdf` and `bug1978317.pdf`, are just as
  slow when drawn the usual way.

Two bugs the check found are fixed:

- Text strokes were drawn too thin, because they went through the font
  matrix.
- Empty glyphs and zero-length butt-capped dashes showed up as dots.

## Not done yet

- **Real AutoCAD exports.** The one real plan in the corpus,
  `22060_A1_01_Plans.pdf`, draws its drawing through tiling patterns that
  hold images, so it falls back to the usual path. Real exports are needed
  to tune the detector and the fallbacks.
- **Images and patterns in scenes.** Title blocks and logos often carry
  them. Supporting them would keep such drawings on the fast path.
- **Form XObjects.** The detector does not count segments inside them yet.
- **The rest of §2.5:**
  - Overview levels.
  - The tile pyramid with the nearest ready level shown scaled.
  - Fast, unsmoothed drawing while a zoom is in motion.
  - Layers.
